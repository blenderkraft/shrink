# Collector Tropes

Stock behaviors of board-game collecting, as mocked on [r/boardgamescirclejerk](https://www.reddit.com/r/boardgamescirclejerk/) and argued in earnest on r/boardgames, BoardGameGeek, and hobby press (Dicebreaker on FOMO, Shelf Gamer and BGG on analysis paralysis, Wargamer on the "wife game").

The subreddit spelling is **boardgames**circlejerk, plural. A lot of these started as sincere posts. The jerk is that reality keeps outrunning the parody. Posts get tagged `/uj` (unjerk, this part is real) and `/rj` (rejerk). When a screenshot is worse than the joke, the comment is just "outjerked."

Use this as the flavor list for Shrink. What the player does, and what they are trying to win, is in [[Game Overview]].

## Shrink is the game

- **NIS.** New-in-shrink. The factory plastic is the component that matters. A played copy is damaged goods.
- **Chinese air.** What you actually collected. The cardboard is a container for sealed atmosphere.
- **Archival bag over the shrink.** Shrink, then a museum sleeve, then the shelf. Opening is a failure state. Returning a copy for "shrink wrap damage" is normal hygiene.
- **Cut, don't peel.** Slice the wrap, lift the lid, leave the plastic on the box so the surface stays mint. You played nothing and still kept the relic.
- **No shrink, no value.** A copy with the "played" look is worthless, unless the poster is doing a bit and claims wear *raises* resale value to "true collectors." Both versions are the same trope.
- **Burn it.** If the wrap is already cut, the only dignified move is to destroy the game. It can no longer be collected.
- **Empty boxes would do.** If the wrap never comes off, the publisher could ship a hollow box and the hobby would not change. The circlejerk's logical endpoint, and something people in the comments immediately try to monetize.
- **Action-figure logic.** The collector compares games to sealed figures or graded comics and cannot understand why people who want to *play* the thing are annoyed. Don Rosa already told this joke about comics.
- **The room of 200.** A whole room, wall to wall, every box sealed, presented as a house tour. Collecting crossed into a storage unit you live next to.

## The shelf

- **Kallax as identity.** The Ikea cube shelf is not furniture. It is the hobby. "Kallax supremacist" is a personality.
- **The empty cube.** One vacant slot at the bottom is not free space. It is a moral failing, a shelf of shame made of air.
- **Room to express themselves.** Games must not be packed tight. They need to breathe. The correct amount of emptiness is a load-bearing argument.
- **Kallax structural integrity.** Bottom-heavy. 3-wide on top, 2-wides under it, never the reverse. People will correct your *photo* of a shelf the way other people correct rules.
- **Lid drift.** Boxes stacked in a cube slowly shove their lids off. There was a real Kickstarter for a Kallax insert whose villain was lid drift. The sub named the genre: Kallaxed and Furious.
- **Billy, not Kallax.** The bookcase heresy. Also: spines in, covers hidden, which is how readers store books and how savages store games.
- **Lights in the cubes.** If it is not lit, it is not a collection. It is a closet.
- **Shelfie.** The photo is the session report. COMC ("check out my collection") is a genre with a required narrative: how I got in, what I culled, what just arrived, please be gentle.
- **Haul post.** Convention weekend, arms full of games, posted before any of them are played. The photo is the trophy.
- **Menu.** A real collection has a menu, like a wine list. Without one you just own boxes.
- **Big box.** Owning the coffin-sized edition is a personality. The follow-up joke is always anatomical. The defense is always "I like them big."
- **Two of everything.** Not a pair. Two copies of every game in an absurd slice of the BGG ranking, curated, with room to express themselves.
- **The dog knows.** If a pet is in the shelfie looking unconvinced, the comments will side with the pet.

## Shame, cellars, and hoards

- **Shelf of shame.** Unplayed games. The hobby's most successful rebrand: a spending problem turned into an endearing identity. People bond over it at conventions.
- **Pile of shame.** Same object, ruder name. Used by people who still feel the guilt.
- **Shelf of opportunity.** The counter-rebrand. Unplayed games are a cellar, not a debt. You pick one to match the day. You will not.
- **Wine cellar.** The respectable metaphor. Stamps, coins, and Funkos get to be collections. Games get called a hoard, and the collector is offended by the double standard.
- **Hoarding.** Buying because you do not have it yet. The next purchase has to clear a tighter bar, and the bar is "do I own this."
- **The dopamine sequence.** New-release email, order, tracking number, unboxing, shelf, nothing. The hit was the purchase. Play was never the product.
- **One more and I'll stop.** The new campaign could be the favorite. The shelf is already stressful. The pledge still goes in.
- **The shelf cap.** A sincere self-control move: the collection may not outgrow this furniture. If something comes in, something leaves. Immediately followed by buying a bigger shelf.
- **Culling as content.** "Games I got rid of" is a video category. The cull is real for some people and a content format for others. The shelf does not get smaller on camera as often as the title promises.
- **500 games, partner can't stop.** The unjerk version. The joke posts sit on top of actual compulsive buying. The sub occasionally drops the bit when a comment describes a marriage breaking over it.

## Cult of the new

- **Cult of the New.** Chase the latest release. The old favorite does not get a second night. The phrase predates Kickstarter. Kickstarter industrialized it.
- **I am immune.** You research. You wait for a second review. You are above hype. Then you look at the shelf: three games sealed for a year, two punched and never played, a pledge that was admired for an evening.
- **BGG Hotness.** A leaderboard that refreshes constantly. Being on it is an event. Owning the current hotness is a personality until next week.
- **Hype train.** Pre-buzz, preview copies, "maybe you shouldn't buy this," said while the comments are already all-in.
- **Fear it won't be reprinted.** Many hobby games get one print run. That fact is real, and it is also the sales pitch. You buy the fear.
- **The reprint that humiliates you.** The exclusive you overpaid for comes back to retail, or the campaign you skipped gets a second run and your FOMO was the product.
- **Gloomhaven as monument.** Sat at #1 on BGG for years, sold in huge numbers, and became the default example of a game people own and do not finish. The box is the hobby.
- **Sampling vs depth.** Two hobbies sharing a shelf. One person wants ten plays of one game. The other wants one play of ten games, then a new ten. Groups break up over this and blame the publisher.
- **Retail is for civilians.** Waiting to buy in a store, after reviews, at a lower price, is treated as not loving the hobby. The hobby is the pledge.

## Kickstarter

- **FOMO.** Fear of missing out. Exclusives, stretch goals, timed promos. If you do not pledge now you will never get the cool bits. Often true just enough to work.
- **All-in.** Every add-on. The pledge manager is a second game, and you are good at it.
- **Deluxification.** Miniatures, metal coins, neoprene, plastic resources, a bigger box. Gameplay is the brochure. The components are the purchase.
- **"Makes a huge fun difference."** The metal coins, specifically. Cited with complete sincerity. The sub treats this sentence as a confession.
- **Stretch goals as plot.** The campaign is a story you follow for a month. The game is the merchandise at the end of the season.
- **Late pledge.** You missed it and still will not wait for retail. There is a product for that feeling.
- **Shut up and take my money.** The pledge is the joke and the behavior.
- **In the mail.** A year of "any day now." When it arrives it is admired and shelved next to the previous arrival.
- **Still in shrink on the secondary market.** The Facebook groups are full of people flipping exclusives they never opened, below cost, calling it a deal.
- **KS exclusive as status.** The retail edition is the compromise copy. Yours has the promo the other table will never see, which you will also never see, because it is in the box.
- **The campaign for a shelf.** Someone Kickstarted an insert so you could remove a box from a Kallax without injury. Hobby hardware for a hobby that is mostly storage.
- **Empty-box business model.** If the all-in is about the shelf photo, the cheapest SKU is a printed carton. The comments reach this conclusion on their own and then keep pledging.

## What a real collection contains

- **Where is Patchwork?** A shelf with no Patchwork is unfinished. Not because you need to play a 15-minute quilt game. Because the shelf failed a test.
- **Multiple Patchworks.** One to play, one to keep, one in another edition, one you do not remember buying. Past a certain number you are not a player who likes Patchwork. You are a person Patchwork happened to.
- **Where is Brass: Birmingham?** If it is the #1 game on BGG and you do not own it, the shelfie is a confession.
- **Have you heard of Arcs?** Said as a personality. Also owned in duplicate by people who have not played it and will say so.
- **Marvel United, the 480th box.** Lifestyle games and miniature lines that never end. You stopped opening them after the second. You did not stop buying.
- **Mage Knight, required.** The relic. If the shelf tour does not include it, someone will ask where it is, hurt.
- **Five copies of a game you hate.** Monopoly variants kept "for the collection" by people who will tell you Monopoly is beneath them. The set is the point.
- **Uno, Munchkin, Exploding Kittens.** Mass-market contamination. Owning them in a shelfie gets "are you okay" and "this is a space for Real Hobby Gamers." The gatekeeping is the joke and also the behavior.
- **At least five Uno variants.** The sarcastic correction. A real gamer would not own one Uno. A real gamer would own a spread. The satire and the shopping habit use the same sentence.
- **Catan is the worst.** Ticket to Ride is forbidden. Risk is a bad game. These opinions arrive as a set, usually right before a recommendation.
- **Gateway shame.** Pandemic and Catan got you here. You are now embarrassed in front of them.
- **Weight snobbery.** A 4.5 weight is treated as better than a 2.0. Weight is rules load, not quality. The shelf still faces the heavy boxes out.
- **Point salad.** An insult when someone else likes it, a diet when you do.
- **Ameritrash vs Euro.** Theme and dice versus brown wood and efficiency. Both sides think the other is playing with toys. Both own the other side's games in shrink.
- **Solo as a lifestyle patch.** The group dried up, so the collection pivoted to solo. Sometimes that is a real hobby. Sometimes it is how a collector avoids admitting the boxes were never for a table.
- **"If I eventually make some friends."** The sealed Kickstarters are an option on a social life that has not started.

## Playing, the embarrassing part

- **Playing is a waste of the investment.** Opened games become the minority. They sit there as a reminder that you once slipped.
- **The 24-hour rule.** The earnest question, asked in a jerk thread: must a game be played within a day of unwrapping? The collector's answer is no. The collector's behavior is never.
- **Crash course before night one.** "It's Great Western Trail tonight, I need a rules video first." Buying, not playing, is already the hobby. Learning is a chore you outsource to a stranger.
- **Teaching a game you have not read.** You own it, so you are the host. The table learns together, badly, from your confidence.
- **Analysis paralysis.** A real pacing problem: one player frozen, everyone else on their phone. Also a badge. Long turns mean you are the smart one.
- **AP is intelligence.** Two graduate degrees, therefore your friends' slow turns are stupidity and your slow turns are optimization. The sub wrote this bit because the earnest version exists.
- **Are you completely sure?** Said to the new player, not to yourself. You win 5% more often and consider this a public service.
- **Hour turns, no eye contact.** The fantasy end state: sit back to back, tap the next player, force a rewind if a move was less than 99% optimal. Ideally do not open the box. Stare at the shrink and declare a winner after 24 hours.
- **"Is it on Kickstarter?"** The only question that matters when someone names a problem, including analysis paralysis.
- **Quarterbacking.** In a co-op, one player plays everyone's turn. They call it helping. The designer problem and the table problem are the same personality.
- **Phones.** Banned, except when someone else is in the tank. Your own tank is thinking.
- **Wrong player count.** Bought the six-player deluxe. The game is better at two. You do not have the two, and you will not play it at six.
- **"We need one more for this to shine."** The missing player is permanent. The game stays sealed until the group becomes theoretical.
- **Play count as morality.** Sorting by least played and feeling accused by the zeros. Ten plays of a favorite is pride. The hobby quietly runs an h-index you are failing.
- **Cult of the old, performed.** "I only play my games." Said by someone with twelve active pledges. Depth is an aesthetic.
- **Unpunching.** Leaving the punchboard intact is purity. Confetti is what careless people have.

## Money and the bit

- **Overpaying is outreach.** Paying two or three times retail, on purpose, for games you will not play. You are an ambassador. The next generation will inherit the boxes and have to pretend.
- **It's an investment.** Sealed games as a portfolio. The market is other people with the same disease.
- **Grailing.** The out-of-print chase. Hunting is the fun. Finding it is a problem, because then it has to go on the shelf and not be played, or the grail is ruined.
- **Extortionate OOP prices.** Cause and symptom of FOMO. The listing is the fear, printed as a number.
- **Math trades and the flip.** The secondary economy: BGG trades, local selloffs, "barely played" (never played). A hobby adjacent to the hobby.
- **Blame the mail.** A package arrived. You have no memory of ordering it. This is a real excuse people have used on their spouses, and a BGG listicle made it a tactic.
- **It was on sale.** The difference is two dollars. Not buying it would be disrespectful to the discount.
- **BGG rank as a receipt.** It is ranked well, therefore the price was correct, therefore owning it is mandatory.
- **Buying the rating.** A 4.5 from a stranger is a purchase order. Taste is outsourced, then the buyer is angry the shelf does not cohere.
- **You will not recoup it.** The shelf is a ledger of review scores you paid full price to obey.

## The Wife and the group

The sub is often mocking posts that treat a spouse as furniture. The trope is the poster, not the spouse. Useful as an NPC, easy to make cruel if the joke punches down. Punch at the collector.

- **The Wife.** A stock character. Not a person with a name. An obstacle to the next pledge, a unit of storage competition, a reason the Kallax cannot have a fifth unit. Pinball has the same character. So does wargaming ("wargame widow").
- **The wife's boyfriend.** The sub's correction. If you are going to talk like she is a prop, at least finish the stock phrase. Sometimes extended into fake polyamory so the collector can get more play time. The convenience is the tell.
- **My wife won't play this.** Means "I want a lighter, shorter, less mean game and I would like to blame someone else for wanting fun." Designers have been hearing it for decades. Almost nobody says "my husband won't play this."
- **She crushed me, so the game is broken.** A drafting game, an obvious bad strategy from the teacher, a win by the person who was supposed to be impressed. The rating must be the game's fault.
- **Better relationship with the Kallax.** The shelf gets more attention than the marriage. The collector is confused why this reads as an insult. He was stating a schedule.
- **Convince her.** A whole folk genre: it was on sale, the rank is high, the Joneses spent more on four-wheeling, the marriage vows covered eleven games in a weekend, the package took five months so it doesn't count as a new purchase.
- **The perfect group.** Exists in theory. Plays the heavy game, on time, no AP, no phones, the right player count, grateful for your teaching. Their absence is why the shrink stays on.
- **One of us.** A new person posts a haul: seven games a week, half still sealed, no Brass, a rules video queued up. The sub chants. BGCJ bingo. The initiation is recognizing yourself and refusing to.
- **Real Hobby Gamers.** A copyright sign is implied. Mass-market games are a border checkpoint. Taste is a membership card you issue to yourself.
- **Polyamory of designers.** The collector loves Reiner Knizia and Uwe Rosenberg at the same time, cheats with Stefan Feld, and occasionally runs off with Cole Wehrle. Designers as partners. Games as relationships. People as optional.

## Reviewers and the church

- **The Dice Tower.** Tom Vasel and company. Rapid-fire reviews, Top 10 lists, a finger rating that became a unit of measurement ("four fingers up"). Big enough that "the Tower" means the channel. Sponsorships, a convention, a cruise.
- **Rahdo runs through it.** Richard Ham, two-handed playthroughs, careful content warnings. "Rahdo Runs Through My Wallet" is the collector version: the run-through is a shopping list.
- **Shut Up & Sit Down.** The other church. Sleeker, meaner, British. The hobby has a mild schism: old-school geek banter versus the newer critical voice. People pick a side and still buy whatever both of them mention.
- **Only trust a paid shill.** Circlejerk logic, aimed at review-for-access and at channels accused of leaning on publishers. If they take money, they have skin in the game. If they love the hobby for free, they are also a shill. The collector needs the review and resents the reviewer.
- **Quackalope.** A recurring sub bogeyman for "the reviewer who is too close to the money." Whether any specific accusation is fair is irrelevant to the trope. The trope is: I need a priest, and I need to believe the other priests are corrupt.
- **Rodney shrine.** Collecting photos of Rodney Smith (Watch It Played) the way other people collect the games he teaches. The teaching video replaces the rulebook, then becomes memorabilia.
- **Brothers Murph.** The third name people expect in a shelfie background, next to Tom and Rodney, like saints in a kitchen.
- **Top 10 of the month.** A content format that manufactures urgency. You do not need the game. You need to have heard the list.
- **Unboxing as play.** The camera comes off when the shrink comes off. There is no part two.
- **Sponsor read.** "This video is made possible by..." The collector hears a review. The channel is running a store. Both things are true often enough to feed the bit.
- **Four fingers.** The rating is a gesture. A game can be loved in the voice and damned by the number of fingers, or the reverse. Viewers buy the fingers.

## Death, display, and the body

- **The dead man's shelf.** An estate photo of unplayed thrift-store games. Comments oscillate between awe at the size and horror at the family who has to liquidate it. "Dicks out to pay respects" is the sub being the sub.
- **Cremated with the games.** Or entombed. Traps that tear the shrink if anyone touches the corpse's boxes. The collection is grave goods. Playing them would be desecration, including by the owner, who also did not play them.
- **Divided among the big boxes.** The will leaves the body packed into Gloomhaven and a campaign box. Joke, but it is the same idea as the sealed room: the games are the remains.
- **Leave them a problem on purpose.** The point of the collection is that descendants have to deal with it. Inheritance as a prank you will not be present for.
- **Tattooed on the carcass.** If they will not bury you with the shelf, wear the shelf. Display has no off switch.

## Language the game should steal

- **The Hobby.** Capitalized. Not a pastime. A nation.
- **Outjerked.** Reality posted a better joke than the sub could write. Highest compliment and a design target.
- **Le whole hobby is a bad experience.** Deliberate bad accent. The pose of a man who has been hurt by cardboard.
- **Normies.** People who play a game because it is fun tonight.
- **Geek cred.** A currency you cannot spend, only display.
- **Paste-on theme.** The mechanics are the real game. The theme is a sticker. Also said by people whose favorite part is the miniature.
- **Deluxified.** Not deluxe. A process that happened to a game that used to be a game.
- **All-in.** A moral condition.
- **NIS.** Already an acronym. Good on a card.
- **COMC.** The shelfie, formalized.
- **AP.** You do not even say the words anymore.
- **/uj.** The tell that the next sentence is the actual confession.

## Tropes that are design fuel

These are the ones with a mechanic sitting inside them. The rest is voice.

- Shrink vs opened is a binary state with social consequences.
- Copy count is not linear. The first Patchwork is ordinary. The fourth is a creed.
- Monopoly is a negative object in public and, in the jerk, sometimes a required negative object. Owning zero is not the same as owning five as a joke.
- Clout (shelfie likes, reviewer agreement, BGG rank obedience) is not the same score as being a collector. Chasing the respectable score is how you lose.
- The spouse, the reviewer, and the subreddit are three different oracles. They leak different rules.
- Empty space, lights, sleeves, and bags are actions that do not touch the game and still change what the shelf *is*.
- A review can move the market without anyone playing. The channel is a player.
- Death, moving house, and a partner's ultimatum are the only events that force a true inventory. Good endgame triggers.
- "Outjerked" means the scoring rule should be weirder than a new player expects, and a little meaner than the player who thought they understood the joke.
